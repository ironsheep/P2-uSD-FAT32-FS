# Socket Timing Characterization — Two microSD Sockets on One P2

Our P2 Edge module's onboard microSD socket and an external header-wired adapter socket were characterized against each other on the P2 itself. The external socket costs one sysclk tick of round-trip delay (~3 ns) and reaches its command-path failure boundary 7.5 MHz lower. Characterizing them also produced the read-path and write-path phase maps that set four of our driver's shipped defaults in v1.8.0.

**Campaign:** 17–19 August 2026.

---

## 1. Applicability

**At the SD specification's 25 MHz, none of this constrains normal operation.** Both sockets carry wide margin there, every card we measured sits inside every passing band, and our shipped defaults sit at or near band centre. If you mount a healthy card at the default speed, there is nothing here you need to act on.

It becomes your problem in four cases:

1. **Porting our driver to different microSD wiring** — a different socket, a ribbon, a breakout adapter, longer traces. §2 gives the magnitude a connector can consume and §8 gives the procedure to measure it.
2. **You have a card that behaves differently in two sockets on the same board.** The effect is real and measurable (§2.3).
3. **Running SPI above 25 MHz.** Since v1.8.0 our driver refuses to without a diagnostic override; §6.3 is why.
4. **Reading our driver source** and requiring the provenance of `align_delay_offset = 5`, the hp = 4 exemption, and the speed clamp.

### 1.1 The two sockets

| | Onboard socket | External socket |
|---|---|---|
| Location | P2 Edge module's own microSD slot | microSD adapter wired to an eval-board header |
| Pins, CS/MOSI/MISO/SCK | **P60 / P59 / P58 / P61** | **P20 / P19 / P18 / P21** (pin group base 16) |
| Path | short PCB traces to the card | header, wiring, connector, adapter PCB |
| Probeable with a logic analyzer | no | yes |
| Also carries | the boot flash; these are the boot pins | nothing; ordinary GPIO |

**Hardware:** the socket that governs our testing cannot be probed — the Edge module's card slot has no exposed test points. The socket that can be probed is the one that is not normally used.

### 1.2 Terms used in this report

**hp — the SCK half-period, in sysclk ticks.** Frequency is expressed this way throughout, because hp is the quantity the hardware actually carries. SCK is generated by a smart pin in `P_TRANSITION` mode and the driver writes hp straight into it — `WXPIN(sck, half_period)` — so the pin toggles once every hp ticks. The SPI frequency is *derived* from that (`spi_freq := clkfreq / (half_period * 2)`), not requested. At 350 MHz sysclk:

| hp | SPI clock | Bit period |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | 12.50 MHz | 28 sysclks |
| 7 | **25.00 MHz** (production default) | 14 sysclks |
| 6 | 29.17 MHz | 12 sysclks |
| 5 | 35.00 MHz | 10 sysclks |
| 4 | 43.75 MHz | 8 sysclks |

One sysclk tick at 350 MHz is **2.857 ns**. hp = 4 is a hardware floor: `applySPISpeed` clamps the half-period there, so `clkfreq / 8` is the fastest SCK any build produces.

**Base period — the same quantity, named from the pin's point of view.** Because the transition pin toggles every hp ticks, hp is the interval on which anything phase-locked to SCK repeats. That is why the write-path map in §4 repeats every hp rather than every bit period.

**Pad — a deliberate stall, in sysclk ticks, inserted to shift one event's phase relative to another.** Both directions have one: `align_delay` on the read path and `tx_align_delay` on the write path, each a cog `WAITX` between starting SCK and starting the streamer. "Pad 4" therefore means a four-tick stall, not a position. Where this report means the physical pin, it says pin.

**Cell — one test condition.** A sweep walks a grid and scores each combination of its swept parameters as a unit. For the frequency sweep a cell is one (frequency × sampling mode × path × socket) combination, scored by eight reads of a known sector; for the write probe it is one (frequency × pad) combination, scored by a write and a readback compare. The **floor cell** is the hp = 4 cell specifically, which §3.7 and §6.2 treat as a special case.

**The dummy-CRC card class.** A conforming card computes a CRC-16 over every data block it returns, and the host validates the read against it. Some cards do not: they return a fixed constant in the CRC position, so the check always passes and a corrupted payload is indistinguishable from a good one. Our driver detects this and raises `CW_NO_DATA_CRC` (`cardWarnings() = $04`). Two of the twenty-six cards in our catalog are in this class — both counterfeit SDSC parts, and both appear in the measurements below. §3.5 is where it changes what the data can prove.

That is a separate gap from the one in §4.4, which affects **every** card and the other direction: in SPI mode a card does not check the CRC the host sends with a write unless CMD59 has enabled it, and our driver never sends CMD59. Reads from a conforming card are CRC-protected. Writes are not protected on any card.

## 2. Findings — the socket difference

### 2.1 The command path fails at a sharp boundary, 7.5 MHz apart

| | Onboard socket | External socket |
|---|---|---|
| Command-path ceiling | **≥ 45.0 MHz** — clean at every reachable cell, including at a 360 MHz overclock | cliff in **(36.25, 37.50] MHz** |
| Margin over the production 25 MHz | ≥ 80% | ≥ 45% |
| Socket delta | reference | **≥ 7.5 MHz** |

The boundary is sharp: 0 errors of 8 at 36.25 MHz, 2 of 8 at 37.50 MHz, with no degraded cell between. The failure signature was byte-identical in every occurrence:

```
4  43_750  ON   |    2   0   0  |    2   0   0
4  43_750  PRE  |    2   0   0  |    2   0   0
```

Exactly 2 of 8 reads fail, on the command column — 0 CRC, 0 data — in both sampling modes and on both paths.

Three conclusions follow directly:

- **The command-response read loses margin first**, not sector data. That read is common code beneath all four mode/path combinations, which is why all four are indistinguishable.
- **The card answers and the answer arrives corrupted.** Failures are `status = -3` (`E_BAD_RESPONSE`), not `-1` (`E_TIMEOUT`). Silence would implicate the outbound path; a corrupted answer implicates the return path.
- **No sampling-point selection recovers it.** On-edge and pre-edge sampling score identically at every cell in every round, consistent with the R1 response arriving bit-slipped once the round trip crosses a bit period (~26.7 ns at 37.5 MHz). A bit landing in the wrong bit position cannot be recovered by choosing an instant within the bit cell.

**Method:** the boundary is carried by four cards across two unrelated families — Gigastone 32 GB `$0000_01C7` and `$0000_01C9`, Lerdisk 1 GB counterfeit SDSC `$0000_01F4`, Cloudisk 2 GB counterfeit SDSC `$0001_9B39` — all clean onboard and all failing externally. The Gigastone pair was run in both socket assignments; the serials exchanged sockets between runs and the failure did not move with them.

**Limitation:** the onboard ceiling is censored, not measured. hp = 4 is the floor at every sysclk, so a faster cell requires overclocking the P2. One bounded overclock to 360 MHz was authorized and run, with every slow cell clean on both sockets as a validity control; the onboard socket passed hp = 4 at 45.0 MHz and the ceiling was not reached. Both figures are one-sided bounds: external cliff ∈ (36.25, 37.50] MHz, onboard ≥ 45.0 MHz, delta ≥ 7.5 MHz.

### 2.2 The socket delta is one sysclk tick

**Finding:** with card, frequency, session and everything else held constant, the read-path passing band's lower edge moves by exactly one.

| Socket | hp / frequency | Path B passing band |
|---|---|---|
| Onboard | 6 / 29.166 MHz | `[0..8]` |
| External | 6 / 29.166 MHz | `[1..8]` |

As a physical quantity: **0 < Δt ≤ ~5.7 ns, most likely ~3 ns.**

**Method:** the run brief originally called for each socket to be swept at its own onset frequency — onboard at 35 MHz, external at 29.17 MHz. That pairing cannot measure a socket delay, because different hp makes the offsets incomparable. A third arm was added so that exactly one variable moves.

**Pitfall:** publish this figure in nanoseconds, never in ticks. The tick count is a property of the sysclk it was measured at; the delay is a property of the wiring. Carried to a 200 MHz design, "one tick" is wrong by a factor of 1.75.

**Limitation:** the delta is bounded, not decomposed. The measurement quantizes to ±1 tick by construction, so one tick means one and not zero and not two; it does not separate 1.0 ns of physical delay from 2.8 ns. Nor does it apportion the delay between wiring length, connector and stub capacitance, and contact resistance. Propagation alone does not plausibly account for ~3 ns — the FR4 rule of thumb is about 150 ps per inch, making flight time over a header and adapter a small fraction of a nanosecond — so capacitive loading and the resulting later threshold crossing is the likely dominant term. That is an inference from magnitude, not a measurement.

### 2.3 Marginal cards encounter the delta before healthy cards do

A card whose own clock-to-output delay is long loses its streamer read alignment one frequency step earlier in the external socket than in the onboard one. The Lerdisk's Path B onset moves from 35.0 MHz onboard to 29.17 MHz externally.

**Pitfall:** presented to a user, this reads as "this card works in one slot and not the other." The card is reporting its own thin margin *plus* the socket delta, and only the sum is observable from outside.

## 3. Findings — read-path phase band

### 3.1 Definition

On the streamer read path, `align_delay` is the cog `WAITX` between starting SCK and starting the streamer; it positions the streamer's sampling grid within the bit period. Our driver computes `align_delay = hp + align_delay_offset`, floored at 2 — the cog requires two sysclks to execute the `WAITX` before `XINIT`.

Below the band, the streamer samples before the card's data is valid. Above it, it samples into the next bit. The band width is the read path's timing margin in sysclk ticks.

### 3.2 The bands

Each arm sweeps offset −3 to +16 and scores each offset by comparing the streamer read against a byte-by-byte reference read of the same sector.

| Card | Socket | hp / frequency | Band | Width | Centre |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gigastone 32 GB | Onboard | 7 / 25.0 MHz | `[-1..11]` | 13 | **5** |
| Gigastone 32 GB | External | 7 / 25.0 MHz | `[-1..12]` | 14 | **5** |
| Gigastone 32 GB | Onboard | 5 / 35.0 MHz | `[1..9]` | 9 | **5** |
| Gigastone 32 GB | External | 6 / 29.166 MHz | `[0..11]` | 12 | **5** |
| Lerdisk 1 GB SDSC | Onboard | 6 / 29.166 MHz | `[0..11]` | 12 | **5** |
| SanDisk MAX Endurance 32 GB | Onboard | 7 / 25.0 MHz | `[-1..12]` | 14 | **5** |
| Samsung EVO Select 128 GB | Onboard | 7 / 25.0 MHz | `[-1..11]` | 13 | **5** |
| Lexar Red 64 GB | Onboard | 7 / 25.0 MHz | `[-1..11]` | 13 | **5** |
| PNY 16 GB | Onboard | 7 / 25.0 MHz | `[-1..11]` | 13 | **5** |

Nine arms, six controller families, two sockets, three frequencies. The lower four rows are SanDisk, Samsung, Longsys and Phison silicon, none of it among the cards that produced the original recommendation. On-edge and pre-edge sampling produced identical bands in every arm: the sampling-mode axis is flat and the alignment axis carries the information.

Two structural properties:

- **Bands narrow as frequency rises, symmetrically about the same centre.** The band closes; it does not slide.
- **The band is a single window, not a periodic one.** The sweep spans up to 20 ticks, more than a full bit period at every frequency measured, and no second band appears; offsets −3 and +11 are exactly one bit period apart at hp = 7 and behave oppositely. The write path (§4) *is* periodic, and the two must not be reasoned about interchangeably.

### 3.3 The band centre is the host's own I/O round trip

**Finding:** every band centres on +5, across six controller families.

**Hardware:** per P2KB (`p2kbArchIoPinTiming`, from the Silicon Doc), an instruction changing an output takes **3 additional sysclks** after the instruction before the pin begins to transition, and an input reaches the cog's view **2–3 sysclks** after the pin depending on the read path. Out and back through the P2's own I/O ring is therefore 5–6 sysclks before any card or wiring is involved. The measured centre sits there.

The dominant term is not the card. The card's clock-to-output delay and the wiring move the band's *edges*, which is why the band narrows with frequency and why the external socket costs a tick; they do not move the centre appreciably. One global default is therefore defensible and per-card calibration is not indicated.

### 3.4 Failure below the band is a one-bit right shift

**Two corruption shapes appear in this report, and telling them apart is the whole of the diagnosis.** A **shift** moves every bit one place and duplicates nothing — the pattern survives intact, displaced; each bit slides one position toward the LSB and the bit that fell off the end of the previous byte slides in at the front. A **smear** duplicates a bit into the position beside it — nothing moves, but every `1` now occupies two places where it occupied one. Displacement versus duplication. A shift is a stream sampled out of step; a smear is a bit that has not finished settling before the next one is sampled. This section is the shift; §4.5 is the smear.

The whole 512-byte payload arrives shifted. Confirmed byte-for-byte rather than inferred from a first-difference report:

- Expected `$C1` uniformly across all 512 bytes; received `$E0` uniformly.
- `$C1` repeated is the bit stream `…11000001 11000001…`. Starting one bit position early takes the previous byte's last bit followed by the first seven of the current byte: `[1][1100000]` = `1110_0000` = `$E0`, for every byte.
- Starting one bit late would produce `$83`, which does not appear.

**Limitation:** because the reference pattern is a uniform repeating byte, any shift of *k* ≡ 1 (mod 8) bits aliases to the same result. The direction is certain; the magnitude is pinned only modulo 8. An absolute count requires a non-uniform reference sector.

**A second shape appears on the read path, at the external socket only.** At that socket's onset frequency the first differing byte read `$E1` rather than `$E0`. A one-bit shift of `$C1` does not produce `$E1` — but a smear does, exactly: `$C1 | ($C1 >> 1)` = `$E1`. Same expected byte, two different corruptions, both on the read path:

| Cell | Socket | Received | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35.0 MHz | onboard | `$E0` | shift |
| 29.17 MHz | external | `$E1` | smear |

If that reading holds, the two shapes are not one-per-path. They are **sampling misalignment → shift** and **edge-rate or settling → smear**, either of which can appear in either direction — and a smear appearing on the external socket specifically is consistent with the extra capacitance §2.2 already names as the likely dominant term in the socket delta.

**Limitation:** this rests on one byte at one cell, with no full dump behind it. It is a hypothesis the arithmetic fits, not a measured result, and §4.5's read/write contrast is stated on the arithmetic rather than on the paths because of it.

**More to do:** the instrument prints only the first differing byte per cell, which is why one byte is all there is. Its full-dump arm prints all 512, and the two shapes are trivially separable across a whole sector: a shift makes every byte `$E0`, a smear makes every byte `$E1`. Re-running that one cell with the dump enabled would therefore settle it outright — and with it, whether the two shapes track *mechanisms* or *paths*, which is a sharper statement of §4.5 and supporting evidence for the capacitive-loading inference in §2.2. It is not required for this study's conclusions: the socket delta, the band centre and the shipped defaults all rest on band edges, which are unaffected by which shape the failure takes.

### 3.5 CRC does not referee this failure on every card

Cards in the dummy-data-CRC quirk class return a constant in the CRC position rather than computing one. A shifted payload from such a card raises no CRC error, and the corruption is silent to any error-driven mechanism.

> **Pitfall:** an alignment mitigation that waits for CRC errors is structurally blind on precisely the silicon most likely to need it.

The characterization tools score these cards by comparing the streamer read against a byte-by-byte smart-pin read of the same sector. Any future auto-calibration must referee the same way.

### 3.6 A reference sector that hid the failure

**Correction:** every pre-fix frequency-sweep Path-B "clean" column is unreliable and was struck from the record. Path A and command-cliff results from the same runs stand, having never depended on reference content. The cause, and why the phase sweep was right throughout, is in §7.1.

### 3.7 Correction — hp = 4 measured in the wrong card state

The band data behind the +5 default was taken at hp = 5, 6 and 7. At hp = 4 the bit period is 8 sysclks and an offset of +5 on top of `align = hp` exceeds it, so the driver carries an exemption: at hp = 4, positive offsets are withheld and `align = hp` is retained. It was recorded as a conservative placeholder pending measurement.

A sweep at hp = 4 on three cards then produced a band of `[2..8]` centred on +5, and the conclusion recorded was that the exemption traded the measured centre for a nearer-edge value and could be deleted.

**Correction:** that conclusion was wrong in two independent respects, both caught before the exemption was removed.

First, an arithmetic error: the exemption produces `align = hp`, which in the sweep's coordinates is **offset 0**, not offset 4. Offset 0 failed on all four cards in that same data.

Second, and decisively, the sweep measured the wrong card state. It reached 43.75 MHz by setting the clock, which leaves the card in default-speed mode driven above specification. In production, hp = 4 arises inside verified CMD6 high-speed mode, where the card's output timing differs by definition.

Re-measured inside verified high-speed mode — the state hp = 4 ships in:

| Card | Band | Centre | Offset 0 (the exemption) | Offset +5 (the global default) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Samsung EVO Select 128 GB | `[-3..4]` | **0** | dead centre | **outside the band** |
| Lexar Red 64 GB | `[-3..4]` | **0** | dead centre | **outside the band** |
| PNY 16 GB | `[-1..5]` | +2 | inside | at the upper edge |

The band moves down by three ticks in the shipping state, and two of three cards exclude +5 outright. The exemption is the correct value and the only one of the two that works on every card measured.

An independent confirmation arrived through instrument failure: before a bench fix, the sweep arm lifted the floor rule *before* negotiating high speed, placing `align = hp + 5` on the CMD6 verify read. On the Lexar that verify failed and the card appeared to decline high speed. It was not declining; +5 does not work on that card at hp = 4, and the driver correctly refused a mode it could not verify.

## 4. Findings — write-path phase map

### 4.1 The write path is periodic, with hp distinct phases

The write path's pad, `tx_align_delay`, is the `WAITX` between the SCK counter reset and `XINIT` at both write-streamer sites. Sweeping it does not produce a window with two edges; it produces a **sawtooth of period hp**.

**Hardware:** SCK starts on the next base-period boundary after the `WYPIN` that launches it, while the streamer start moves continuously with the pad. Advancing the pad by hp ticks therefore returns the same phase relationship. Only hp distinct phases exist regardless of sweep range: pads 2 through 30 at hp = 7 walk the same seven phases four times.

Exactly one phase per period fails.

### 4.2 The teeth

| hp | SPI | Failing pads | Residue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 12.5 MHz | 8, 22 | ≡ 8 (mod 14) |
| 7 | 25.0 MHz | 8, 15, 22, 29 | ≡ 1 (mod 7) |
| 5 | 35.0 MHz | **4, 9** | ≡ 4 (mod 5) |
| 4 | 43.75 MHz | 2, 6, 10 | ≡ 2 (mod 4) |

**Limitation:** the spacing is always hp, but the phase of the tooth is not predictable from hp. Four frequencies give four residues and no formula worth applying. Each frequency requires its own measurement.

### 4.3 The losing phase is driver-side

The original characterization was performed on one card, supporting the reading that it reflected that card's input timing. A five-card survey does not support it:

| Card | Silicon | Tooth | Failing pads at hp = 7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gigastone 32 GB `$0000_01C7` | Transcend, SD 3.x | no | passing band 2..30 |
| Gigastone Camera Plus 64 GB `$0000_0F14` | ASTC, SD 6.x | **yes** | 8, 15, 22, 29 |
| SanDisk Extreme PRO 64 GB `$A345_3C0E` | SanDisk, SD 5.x | **yes** | 8, 15, 22, 29 |
| Lexar Blue 128 GB `$3449_0F1E` | Longsys, 2025 | no | passing band 2..30 |
| WD Purple QD101 64 GB `$EEBA_D6C0` | SanDisk WX64G, SD 6.x | **yes** | 8, 15, 22, 29 |

Three of five express it, and every card that does expresses it at identical pads — across three controller families, specification revisions SD 5.x and 6.x, and manufacture dates six years apart. A card timing property would differ between parts that different.

> The losing phase is a fixed, driver-side phase relationship in our own write path. It occurs at the same pads on every card. What varies card to card is whether that card's input margin tolerates it.

One driver-side change therefore covers the whole population, and per-card calibration is not required.

### 4.4 The shipped pad lands on a tooth at 35 MHz

`tx_align_delay = 4` was selected as the maximal distance from the losing phase at hp = 7 — distance 3 on both sides — and remains correct at 25 MHz on every card surveyed. At hp = 5 the teeth are at pads 4 and 9, and the shipped default is pad 4. On three of five cards that produces `DATA_MISMATCH`, 512 bytes of 512 wrong: silent whole-sector write corruption, with the write returning success and the damage visible only on readback.

**Finding:** our driver computes a real CRC-16 over the write buffer, transmits it after the streamed payload, and decodes the card's data-response token, branching on the CRC-error and write-error codes. A card validating the data-block CRC would therefore have rejected a block whose payload no longer matched the CRC following it. These cards accepted it. SPI-mode CRC checking is off at reset and we never send CMD59 to enable it, so the write path has no referee and readback is the only detector.

Distance from a cliff computed at one frequency does not transfer to another.

### 4.5 The write corruption is bit smearing, not a bit shift

This is the second of the two shapes introduced in §3.4 — duplication, not displacement. At every failing pad the corrupted byte is exactly `exp | (exp >> 1)`:

| Pad | Expected | Received | `exp \| (exp>>1)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `$C2` | `$E3` | `$C2 \| $61` = `$E3` |
| 6 | `$46` | `$67` | `$46 \| $23` = `$67` |
| 10 | `$CA` | `$EF` | `$CA \| $65` = `$EF` |

Every `1` bit also appears in the next-lower bit position — the preceding transmitted bit bleeding into the following one. That is a settling or edge-rate signature, not the stream sliding by a position. A one-bit shift produces `exp >> 1` with a carry-in, which is what the read path produces (`$C1` → `$E0`; a smear would give `$E1`) and what the write path does not.

The signature reproduces on a second controller at lower severity: same failing pads, same smear direction, one bit smeared where the other card smeared several (379–380 bytes of 512 affected against 496). A shift would corrupt both streams identically.

> **Pitfall:** the two shapes have different arithmetic and neither may be diagnosed using the other's. What is established is that the *shapes* differ; binding one shape to one path is a step further than the evidence goes — see §3.4, where the read path appears to produce both.

### 4.6 hp = 4, measured in the shipping state

Measured inside verified high-speed mode, four repeats byte-identical, then replicated on two further cards. Teeth at pads 2, 6, 10 — period 4, residue ≡ 2 (mod 4) — on two of the three cards; the third expresses no tooth. **Pad 4 passes on all three**, two pads clear of the nearest tooth wherever one exists. An independent check agrees: a benchmark verifying every write found 24 of 24 clean at high speed across three cards.

The write default is therefore established safe at both frequencies production can reach, on three cards rather than one.


## 5. Method

### 5.1 The instrument is the P2, and the tier that answered was the cheap one

The plan specified three tiers. Tier 1 was a pass/fail margin sweep assembled from driver machinery that already existed. Tier 2 was an XOR skew probe reconfiguring the MISO smart pin mid-transaction to time the returning edge directly, run at six sysclk frequencies to defeat quantization. Tier 3 was edge-rate profiling against the pins' internal comparator DACs.

The tiering existed to support a calibration transfer: measure the external socket with both the logic analyzer and the on-die instrument, reconcile them, then point the calibrated instrument at the socket the analyzer cannot reach.

**Tier 1 answered the question. Tier 2 was never built and no logic analyzer was attached.**

The on-die instrument has one property the analyzer does not: it is symmetric. The same instrument, through the same input path, sees both sockets. Fixed internal offsets — synchronizer latency, smart-pin pipeline, input buffer — are identical for both sockets and cancel on subtraction. With the card held constant, any difference is attributable to socket and wiring.

**Limitation:** no external instrument has checked these figures. The calibration transfer that motivated the plan was never executed. Every number rests on the P2 measuring itself. The differential measurements are robust to that; the absolute round-trip delay has never been traced to an oscilloscope or analyzer.

### 5.2 The measurand

The quantity governing read correctness is the round-trip data-valid delay at the P2 pin: the SCK edge leaves the P2 pin, propagates through socket and wiring, the card launches MISO after its clock-to-output delay (t_ODLY), the signal returns through socket and wiring, and MISO crosses the P2's input threshold.

**Limitation:** the public specification does not bound t_ODLY. The SD Physical Layer *Simplified* Specification removes the relevant tables — spec §6.6.6 (Bus Timing, Default) retains the figures but not the values, spec §6.6.7 (High-Speed Mode) is "a blank in the Simplified Specification", and spec §7.5.4's Table 7-7 (SPI Timing Values) is removed. A host designer working from the public specification cannot compute this budget.

### 5.3 Instruments and scoring

Our driver reads MISO by two paths, which fail differently and are scored separately in every cell.

- **Path A — smart-pin direct (`sp_transfer_8`).** Single-byte transfers: command responses, register reads, CRC bytes, token polling. Sample point set by the MISO smart pin's mode bit.
- **Path B — streamer DMA.** 512-byte sector transfers. Sample point set by `align_delay`.

`SD_socket_shmoo.spin2` walks frequency × sampling mode × path × socket in one powered session, reading a known sector eight times per cell and scoring command errors, CRC errors and data mismatches separately. `SD_phase_sweep_test.spin2` holds frequency and walks the `align_delay` offset from −3 to +16, reporting the passing band.

### 5.4 Separating card from socket

Populating both sockets simultaneously measures two different cards, confounding card with socket. The protocol is a crossed 2×2: run the full matrix, exchange the two cards between sockets, run again.

The primary pair was matched — two Gigastone 32 GB SDHC cards with consecutive serials from the same production reel — so the card term was small before the exchange removed the remainder. Later rounds replicated across unrelated families.

Every phase of every run prints the CID serial of the card in the socket under test. A card exchange is verified from the transcript rather than from bench notes.

### 5.5 The sysclk ladder

Round 1 bracketed the external failure between hp = 5 (35.0 MHz, clean) and hp = 4 (43.75 MHz, failing) — an 8.75 MHz interval with no finer step available, hp = 4 being the floor. Holding hp = 4 and rebuilding at a lower sysclk places the fastest cell between the 350 MHz grid points:

| sysclk | hp = 4 cell |
|---|---|
| 290 MHz | 36.25 MHz |
| 300 MHz | 37.50 MHz |
| 320 MHz | 40.00 MHz |
| 336 MHz | 42.00 MHz |
| 350 MHz | 43.75 MHz |

**Pitfall:** two sysclks whose tick grids coincide yield repeated behavior rather than new information. Boundaries coincide where `n·T_i = m·T_j`; with the frequency ratio `p/q` in lowest terms, the first coincidence sits at `p/f_i`. Pairs such as 175/350 (1:2, grids nest completely), 200/300 (2:3, first coincidence at 10 ns) and 280/350 (4:5, 14.3 ns) carry redundant information. The selection rule is `p/f_i ≥ 20 ns` for every pair. The ladder above satisfies it: worst pair 300/350 = 6:7 at exactly 20 ns, every other pair at ≥ 50 ns.

## 6. Response — what changed in the driver

Five changes came out of the campaign; two more came out of certifying them.

| # | Change | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read `align_delay_offset` default 0 → **+5** | Nine passing-band arms, six card families, all centred on +5 (§3.3) |
| 2 | **hp = 4 floor-cell rule** — positive offsets withheld at hp = 4 | +5 exceeds the bit period there; measured inside high-speed mode, offset 0 is the band centre (§3.7) |
| 3 | **Production speed bound** in `clampUserSpeed()` | The write tooth lands on the shipped pad above 25 MHz (§4.4) |
| 4 | `debugSetOverspeedAllowed()`, `debugSetAlignFloorRuleEnabled()`, `debugSetAlignDelayOffset` clamp widened to `[-8, +16]` | Characterization tools must reach what production must not; the band top was censored by the old clamp |
| 5 | `hs_mode_active` explicit state; `isHighSpeedActive()` reports the card's mode | Found during certification (§6.4) |
| 6 | CMD6 switch-back on every high-speed exit | Found during certification (§6.4) |

### 6.1 Read alignment moved to the measured centre

Through v1.7.0 the shipped `align_delay` was `hp + 0` — the band's lower edge, and at elevated frequencies on or outside it. The default is now `hp + 5`.

The effect is observable rather than inferred. Re-running the frequency sweep afterwards, the streamer read boundary moved a full frequency step on both sockets:

| Socket | Path B boundary before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Onboard | 29.166 MHz | **35.0 MHz** |
| External | 29.166 MHz | **35.0 MHz** |

Both sockets now share the same read-streamer boundary. With alignment centred, the read path's limit is card- and driver-bound rather than socket-bound: the socket difference resides in the command path. The one-tick cost remains real but no longer determines where reads fail, because the default no longer sits one tick from an edge.

**Cost:** the driver no longer writes the same bytes to the smart pins as before the alignment work. Bit-for-bit equivalence with pre-Phase-2/3 behavior was a deliberate v1.7.0 property, exchanged for centring the read alignment on its measured band.

### 6.2 The hp = 4 exemption

`effectiveAlignDelay()` is the single computation shared by both burst sites and the diagnostic getter:

```spin2
  if spi_period >= 5 or floor_rule_disabled
    delay := spi_period + align_delay_offset
  else
    delay := spi_period + (align_delay_offset <# 0)     ' hp=4: positive offsets withheld
  if delay < 2
    delay := 2                                          ' WAITX floor
```

**Pitfall:** the source comment records that this rule was introduced as a conservative placeholder and has since been measured correct. Deleting it on the strength of the §3.7 first measurement would have broken two of three cards.

### 6.3 The write tooth is bounded by clamping speed, not by moving the pad

Three options were considered:

1. Clamp or warn on SPI targets above the card's declared ceiling.
2. Make the pad frequency-aware — a per-hp table avoiding the losing residue. The only option preserving writes above 25 MHz.
3. Document the constraint and accept it.

Option 1 was selected: above-specification SPI is diagnostic and developer territory rather than a production feature. Option 2 was rejected as machinery supporting a capability our driver does not offer. Option 3 was rejected because the bound would exist only in prose.

`clampUserSpeed()` bounds user `setSPISpeed()` requests at the card's declared `TRAN_SPEED` capped at 25 MHz, lifted to 50 MHz only while verified CMD6 high-speed mode is active. Internal speed changes — the 400 kHz init, `setOptimalSpeed()`, the high-speed path — are unaffected. Characterization tools recover the full range through `debugSetOverspeedAllowed()`.

**Finding:** our clamp is the specification's own envelope, not a bench heuristic. Part 1 Physical Layer Simplified Specification v9.10 states that for current SD memory cards the `TRAN_SPEED` field "shall be always" `032h`, "which is equal to 25 MHz - the mandatory maximum operating frequency of SD Memory Card", and `05Ah` = 50 MHz in high-speed mode, reverting to `032h` when CMD6 or CMD0 returns the card to default timing (Table 5-6 note). Default Speed mode is defined as 3.3 V signalling up to 25 MHz and High Speed mode up to 50 MHz. Spec §7.8 states that in SPI mode bus timing "is identical to SD mode", and UHS-I "is provided in SD mode but not in SPI mode". The configuration that lands on the tooth is therefore out of specification on any SD card in SPI mode.

### 6.4 Certification

The first full-regression attempt regressed `SD_RT_speed_tests`: at hp = 4 with the new +5 offset applied, the CMD6 high-speed verify read failed — the same failure §3.7 later explained. That produced the floor-cell rule.

The second attempt exposed an older defect: after a verified high-speed switch followed by a hand-set clock, the card was left in CMD6 high-speed mode while the host had returned to default timing, and every subsequent operation returned `E_IO_ERROR`. The fix hooks a CMD6 switch-back onto every high-speed exit path.

A third finding followed. `isHighSpeedActive()` inferred high-speed mode from a 50 MHz clock threshold. At 350 MHz sysclk a successful high-speed negotiation resolves to hp = 4 = 43.75 MHz, below that threshold, so the getter returned FALSE while the card was in high-speed mode. It now reports the card's mode from explicit state. This is a breaking change to a public getter and ships as one in v1.8.0.

The third attempt was clean: 530 tests across 27 suites, 0 failures, closing filesystem audit clean, on the onboard socket. <!-- doc-count: historical --> (That was the roster at the time; the suite has since grown, and test totals also vary with the card's cluster geometry.) A second full sweep on a 119 GB / 64-sectors-per-cluster geometry also passed.

### 6.5 Rejected alternatives

- **Reactive, CRC-driven adaptation** — structurally blind on dummy-CRC cards (§3.5).
- **Per-card CID tables or per-socket pin tables** — they encode our one bench and do not generalize.
- **Per-vendor read calibration** — nine arms across six controller families all centre on +5.
- **Mount-time auto-calibration** — the intended destination, and not built. It would slow-read a sector as ground truth, streamer-read at several offsets, and sit mid-band, handling any card × socket × sysclk × frequency combination including unmeasured ones, working on dummy-CRC cards because the referee is a data compare, at a cost of microseconds once per mount. Building it would remove the dependence on a single global default being right for silicon we have not met. Parked, not rejected: the measured defaults cover every card in the catalog, so it buys generality rather than correcting a known fault.

## 7. What we concluded and later reversed

Two conclusions in this campaign were recorded, acted on as working assumptions, and later refuted. They are collected here rather than left in the findings, because the findings stand without them. One correction is *not* here — the hp = 4 exemption in §3.7 — because that fact is indefensible without the reasoning that produced it, and a reader who meets the exemption without it will conclude, as we did, that it should be deleted.

### 7.1 Reference-content aliasing

**Correction:** for one round the frequency sweep and the phase sweep disagreed about the same nominal configuration. The frequency sweep reported the Gigastone reading cleanly onboard at 35 MHz at offset 0; the phase sweep reported offset 0 failing there and offset +1 passing.

The disagreement was not drift. Both tools were re-run two minutes apart on one seated card in one session, and it reproduced exactly.

The cause was reference-content aliasing. The frequency sweep compared against a reference sector on a freshly formatted card whose content was a single repeated byte, and a one-bit shift of that content reproduces itself byte-for-byte including its CRC. The frequency sweep's Path B was blind precisely where the phase sweep — using the structured sector-0 MBR — observed the shift. The frequency sweep now scans for a shift-distinguishable reference, falls back to sector 0, and prints which sector it selected.

Two consequences: the phase sweep was correct throughout, and its recommended default is the one that shipped; and **every pre-fix frequency-sweep Path-B "clean" column is unreliable and was struck from the record.** Path A and command-cliff results from the same runs stand, having never depended on reference content.

### 7.2 The write probe's green results were not evidence

Across three rounds the write probe ran approximately 336 cells on four cards of two families, including the two counterfeit cards whose write path was under investigation, and produced zero `WRITE_ERR`, zero `READBACK_ERR`, zero `DATA_MISMATCH`. Every defined failure mode was unreached.

Two readings fit that data equally: the write path is broadly tolerant, or the probe cannot report a failure. The record refused to select:

> Do not record "the write path is healthy" from this data. The correct statement is *the probe reported no failures, and the probe has never been shown capable of reporting one.*

Two deliberately-broken cells established that the detection paths fire:

```
1) wrong-expectation compare reports mismatch:   INSTRUMENT PASS
2) absurd-LBA write returns error (status=-7):   INSTRUMENT PASS
SELFTEST VERDICT: detection paths PROVEN -- green grids are evidence
```

A second gap sat beneath the first: the probe's phase structure had never run the one condition already known to fail — the original characterization's onboard-socket single-block-write sweep. A new arm ran exactly that condition and reported `DATA_MISMATCH diffs=512` at the tooth. Detection is proven both synthetically and against a real defect.

## 8. Limitations

Collected from the sections above, plus those not stated elsewhere.

- **The onboard command-path ceiling is unmeasured** — ≥ 45.0 MHz, one-sided, censored by the P2's rated sysclk (§2.1).
- **No external instrument has verified any figure here** (§5.1).
- **The socket delta is bounded, not decomposed** (§2.2).
- **One board, one adapter.** The socket delta is a property of these two sockets. Read ~3 ns as an order of magnitude for a header-and-adapter path, not as a constant.
- **One of the three ways hp = 4 arises has never been measured.** The read band at hp = 4 is measured inside verified CMD6 high-speed mode, and separately in default-speed mode driven above specification at 43.75 MHz. Neither is the third configuration our driver permits: a low-sysclk build where 25 MHz itself resolves to hp = 4 — a 200 MHz sysclk, for instance. That configuration is in specification, is reachable by any caller setting a lower clock, has the floor-cell rule applied to it, and has not been swept.

  **More to do:** hp is `clkfreq / (2 × SPI)`, so lowering the system clock reaches hp = 4 at an ordinary 25 MHz — a 200 MHz build does exactly that — without leaving the specification or entering high-speed mode. Sweeping the read band there is the only way to see the floor cell in its third state, and it is a direct comparison: the same offset sweep as §3.7, run at 200 MHz instead of 350. The rule is already measured correct inside verified high-speed mode, so this is a coverage question rather than an open doubt, and it matters to a user running a lower system clock rather than to anything concluded here.
- **The write tooth's phase is tabulated, not predicted** (§4.2). Any change raising production SPI speed must re-derive the pad for its hp; our driver source states this at the declaration.
- **The write tooth is bounded by policy and specification, not by physics.** Since v1.8.0 a production caller cannot reach the failing configuration. That is a clamp and a specification argument, not a demonstration that the phase relationship is harmless. A card declaring a higher `TRAN_SPEED` in SPI mode would move the bound.
- **The read band is characterized on one sampling structure.** On-edge and pre-edge gave identical bands in every arm, so the sampling-mode axis contributed nothing and was never able to falsify anything.

## 9. Procedure — measuring this on other hardware

You need no special equipment — the P2 is the instrument — and one card you are willing to reformat.

**Step 1. Establish whether you have a socket difference at all.** Run the same program on both sockets with the same card at 25 MHz. If both are clean, you have margin, and everything below is characterization rather than debugging.

**Step 2. Sweep frequency and record which error column moves.** Walk hp from 14 down to 4, scoring reads per cell. Record the frequency at which errors first appear and the column they appear in: command errors indicate the response path bit-slipping, data errors indicate the streamer's sampling point leaving the window.

**Step 3. Exchange the cards between sockets and repeat.** This is the step that turns an anecdote into a measurement. A boundary that stays with the socket is a socket property; one that follows the card is a card property, which is also worth knowing.

**Step 4. Sweep the alignment offset at one fixed frequency on both sockets.** `debugSetAlignDelayOffset()` under `SD_INCLUDE_DEBUG` walks the read path's phase pad. The passing band's lower edge moving by one between two sockets, at the same frequency on the same card, is the socket's delay in sysclk ticks. Multiply by your tick period and state the result in nanoseconds.

**Step 5. Score against a slow reference read, not against CRC.** If your card is in the dummy-CRC class, CRC never fires and every sweep reads clean. Read the sector byte-by-byte at low speed and compare against that. And check that your reference sector's content is actually shift-distinguishable — ours was not, for a whole round (§7.1).

**Step 6. Establish that the instrument can report a failure before trusting that it reported none.** Supply a deliberately wrong expectation and a deliberately bad configuration.

**Step 7. If you are selecting an alignment default for your own port.** Expect the centre near your host's own I/O round trip — on the P2 that is 5–6 sysclks, and the measured centre is +5 — and take the centre of the narrowest band you measure, not the midpoint of the widest.

## 10. Method notes

**Measure in the state that ships.** Two conclusions in this campaign inverted when re-measured in the shipping state: reference-content aliasing (§7.1) and the hp = 4 band (§3.7). A measurement reaching its target by a shortcut states which state it was taken in, or it does not count.

**Establish that an instrument can fail before trusting that it passed** (§7.2).

**Design the third arm.** Two arms taken at each socket's own onset frequency cannot measure a socket delay however natural the pairing appears (§2.2).

**Remove confounds by protocol, not by argument.** The 2×2 exchange costs one run and removes the card term. Asserting that two cards are similar enough does not.

**Re-run a disagreement in one session before explaining it.** Drift and staleness are the available explanations for two instruments contradicting each other; two minutes apart on one seated card removes both, and the losing instrument's failure mode was the finding (§7.1).

**Record the retroactive consequence.** When the aliasing was understood, a class of previously recorded results had to be struck. Recording which results, and why, is what prevents their later citation.

**Card-specific and driver-specific are indistinguishable until surveyed.** One card's tooth reads as one card's problem; five cards read as a driver-side phase relationship that some cards tolerate (§4.3).
